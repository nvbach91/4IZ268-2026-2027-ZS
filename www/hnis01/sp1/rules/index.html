Creation

Class

3 Main Archetypes: While D&D has 12 main classes, they generally fall into three gameplay roles:

  • Martial (Martial Combatants): Fighter, Barbarian, Rogue, Monk. They rely on physical weapons, armor, agility, or raw strength to deal and take damage.
  • Spellcasters (Magic Users): Wizard, Sorcerer, Cleric, Druid, Bard, Warlock. They use magic to deal massive area damage, heal allies, control the battlefield, or utility spells.
  • Hybrid (Half-Casters): Paladin, Ranger (and Artificer). They combine physical weapon combat with supporting spellcasting.
Race (Species)

Your race determines your physical appearance, innate traits, and heritage (e.g., Human, Elf, Dwarf, Halfling, Orc). It provides special traits such as Darkvision (ability to see in the dark), movement speed (usually 30 ft.), and racial features (like an Elf's resistance to charm magic).

Background

Your background describes where your character comes from before becoming an adventurer (e.g., Acolyte, Criminal, Folk Hero, Noble, Soldier). It gives you:

  • Proficiency in 2 Skills (e.g., Stealth, Athletics).
  • Tool proficiencies or starting languages.
  • Starting equipment and a unique narrative feature (like free lodging at temples).
Alignment

A two-word guide for your character's moral compass across two axes:

  • Lawful / Neutral / Chaotic: How much you respect rules, tradition, and authority.
  • Good / Neutral / Evil: How much you value the life and well-being of others versus your self-interest.

Example: Lawful Good plays by the rules to do right by people; Chaotic Neutral follows their own freedom first.

Ideals, Bonds, and Flaws

Roleplay hooks that bring your character to life:

  • Ideals: The core beliefs or principles that drive you (e.g., "Freedom for all").
  • Bonds: Your ties to people, places, or events in the world (e.g., "I must save my hometown").
  • Flaws: A vulnerability, vice, or weakness that can get you into trouble (e.g., "I can't resist a shiny coin").
Ability Scores

The 6 core attributes measuring your physical and mental traits (ranging from 3 to 20, with 10–11 being average human level):

  • Strength (STR): Physical power, athletics, melee damage.
  • Dexterity (DEX): Agility, reflexes, stealth, armor class (AC).
  • Constitution (CON): Health points (HP), stamina, concentration.
  • Intelligence (INT): Logic, memory, Arcana, History.
  • Wisdom (WIS): Perception, insight, intuition, survival.
  • Charisma (CHA): Force of personality, persuasion, deception.

Each score yields an Ability Modifier (e.g., a score of 14 gives a +2 modifier), which is what you add to your d20 dice rolls.

Spells

If your class casts spells, keep these core terms in mind:

  • Cantrips: Level-0 spells that can be cast unlimited times without using spell slots (e.g., Fire Bolt, Light).
  • Known / Prepared Spells: The specific list of higher-level spells available for you to cast each day.
  • Spellcasting Ability: The attribute used for your spell rolls (Wizard = INT, Cleric = WIS, Bard/Sorcerer/Warlock = CHA).

Leveling

Health (Hit Points)

When you level up, your maximum HP increases. You gain additional HP equal to your class's Hit Die + your Constitution Modifier.

  • You can either roll your Hit Die (e.g., 1d8, 1d10) for the new amount or take the average fixed value listed in your class table (e.g., 5 for a d8 hit die).
Ability Score Improvement (ASI)

At certain levels (typically 4, 8, 12, 16, and 19), your character gets to improve their stats:

  • Increase one Ability Score by +2, OR increase two different Ability Scores by +1 each.
  • Optional Rule: You can forgo an ASI to select a Feat (a special talent like War Caster or Sharpshooter).
Subclass

A specialization within your main class that unlocks unique abilities (e.g., a Fighter choosing Champion vs. Battle Master, or a Wizard choosing School of Evocation).

  • Most classes pick their subclass at Level 3 (Sorcerers and Warlocks choose at Level 1, Wizards and Druids at Level 2).
Multiclassing

Instead of taking a level in your primary class when leveling up, you take a level in a new class (e.g., Level 4 Fighter / Level 1 Rogue):

  • You must meet the minimum stat requirement (usually score of 13+) in both your current class and your new class.
  • You gain the core features of the new level 1 class, but lose out on reaching higher-level features of your main class as quickly.
Spell Slots

Spell slots act as "currency" or "ammo" to cast your 1st-level and higher spells. As you level up, you gain more slots and access to higher slot levels (up to 9th level):

  • Casting a spell consumes a slot of that spell's level or higher.
  • Expended spell slots are fully recovered after completing a Long Rest (8 hours of rest).
